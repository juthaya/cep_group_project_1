# How Much Does AI Actually Cost?

The cost of artificial intelligence varies depending on whether someone uses a free chatbot, integrates AI into a business, or develops a large AI model. Everyday tools can be free or cost around $20 per month, while developing advanced AI systems can cost hundreds of millions of dollars or more.

## 1. Everyday Use: Free AI and Subscriptions

**Free AI Tools:** Many AI assistants offer free versions for writing, studying, research, and answering questions, although usage limits may apply.

**Premium Subscriptions:** Services such as ChatGPT Plus and Claude Pro have offered plans around $20 per month in the United States. One subscription costs approximately $240 annually before taxes.

**Consumer Spending:** Paying for multiple AI subscriptions can quickly increase expenses, while free versions may be sufficient for casual users.

## 2. Business Use: API Costs and Implementation

**AI for Businesses:** Companies use AI for customer service, marketing, data analysis, and document processing. Costs depend on the tools and amount of usage.

**API Pricing:** Businesses often pay according to the number of tokens processed. Smaller models generally cost less than advanced models.

For example, a business making 10,000 requests monthly, each using 1,000 input tokens and generating 500 output tokens, could pay around $3.50 to $70 in API fees under illustrative pricing assumptions.

**Hidden Expenses:** Software integration, developers, hosting, employee training, cybersecurity, and human oversight can make implementation considerably more expensive.

## 3. The Big Money: Training AI Models

**Model Development:** Training advanced AI models requires specialised chips, computing infrastructure, electricity, and skilled researchers. These expenses can reach hundreds of millions of dollars.

**Data Centres:** AI also requires substantial investment in data centres, cooling systems, and power infrastructure. Goldman Sachs Research projected that global data centre power demand could increase by 170% between 2025 and 2030.

**Why It Costs So Much:** Hardware, research staff, experimentation, and ongoing infrastructure make developing advanced AI far more expensive than using an existing model.

## 4. Why AI Is Becoming Cheaper

**Lower Operating Costs:** Competition, smaller models, and improved technology can reduce the cost of running AI systems.

**Open Weight Models:** Businesses can run certain models on their own infrastructure, potentially reducing API expenses. However, hardware, electricity, and maintenance still cost money.

**Greater Accessibility:** Falling prices allow more individuals and smaller businesses to use AI, although increased usage can raise total spending.

## 5. Is AI Worth the Cost?

**Productivity and Savings:** AI can reduce time spent on repetitive tasks, improve efficiency, and support business growth.

**The Risk of Overspending:** Incorrect outputs, training, integration, and human verification can reduce potential savings.

**Measuring Value:** Businesses should compare the total cost of AI with the money saved, productivity gained, and additional revenue generated.

Ultimately, AI is worth its cost when the value it creates exceeds the expense of developing, operating, and maintaining it.

# DeepSeek vs OpenAI & The Market Disruption

**1. The Launch Shockwave (January 2025)**

**Cost Efficiency Breakthrough:** DeepSeek launched an open reasoning large language model (LLM) developed at a reported ~95% discount to train and operate compared to OpenAI’s cutting-edge models
. It achieved comparable performance in math, coding, and reasoning using older chips and architectural efficiency rather than brute-force hardware clusters

**Dethroning ChatGPT:** Following its release, the DeepSeek app dethroned ChatGPT at the top of worldwide mobile app store charts

**The "Sputnik Moment":** Industry analysts described the launch as a "Sputnik moment" that disrupted US tech dominance and turned the global AI landscape into a bipolar US-China dynamic

**2. The Tech & Semiconductor Market Crash**

Record Nvidia Wipeout: On January 27, 2025, Nvidia suffered a ~$600 billion single-day market value loss — the largest one-day wipeout in US stock market history
. Its stock fell 11.7% pre-market

**Global Semiconductor Slide:** European chipmakers plummeted: ASML fell ~9.5%, BE Semiconductor dropped over 11%, ASM International slid ~10%, and Arm Holdings dropped ~10%

**Big Tech Drops:** Major hyperscalers and AI leaders took immediate hits: Microsoft dropped 5.9%, Alphabet 3.5%, and Meta 3.2%

**Why Markets Panicked:** The market realized that frontier AI might be significantly less capital-intensive and compute-dependent than previously assumed, threatening the premium valuations of chipmakers and infrastructure providers

**3. The 2026 Price War & "Intelligence Price Band"**

**OpenAI Pricing Response:** To stay competitive against open models, OpenAI slashed its GPT-5.6 Luna API pricing by ~80%
. This established a pricing ceiling across the industry

**DeepSeek Compute Limits:** As compute demand surged, DeepSeek subsequently raised its V4 API prices by 50% to 1,100% due to capacity bottlenecks
. This created a pricing floor

**Market Equilibrium:** Intelligence now trades in a bounded price band, capped from above by open-weight competition and supported from below by hardware and power scarcity

# What Is The Impact of AI On The Economy?

**1. Everyday Use: AI as a Core Macro Utility**

**From Novelty to Infrastructure:** AI has transitioned from an experimental software novelty into a foundational economic utility, with 77% of organizations using or actively exploring AI and 83% designating it as a top business priority

**Mass Consumer Adoption:** Generative AI achieved 53% global adoption in just three years—spreading faster than either the personal computer or the internet
. Today, over 55% to 77% of consumers regularly interact with AI tools in their daily lives, generating $172 billion in annual US consumer surplus value

**Trillion-Dollar Macro Footprint:** Worldwide AI-related investment is projected to surpass $1 Trillion in 2026 ($581 billion in the US alone)
. AI capital expenditure is expected to reach 1.8% of US GDP in 2026 and climb to 2.8% by 2028, placing it on par with major historical technology buildouts like the internet

**2. Real-World Sectors:** Concrete Impact on Banks & Stores
Banking & Financial Institutions
Automated Compliance & Risk: 89% of financial institutions monitor regulatory compliance in real time using AI, reducing compliance-related operational costs by 19% and cutting audit preparation times by 35%

**Fraud Prevention:** AI-driven fraud detection systems are deployed across 87% of global banks, intercepting 92% of fraudulent attempts before approval and reducing false alerts by up to 80%

**Credit Scoring & Underwriting:** AI is now involved in over 70% of loan underwriting decisions among top-tier US banks. AI models accelerate loan processing times by 25% (often under 6 minutes) while improving approval accuracy by 34%

**Scalable Customer Service:** Chatbots and virtual assistants handle 70% to 85% of Tier 1 banking queries. For instance, Bank of America’s Erica assistant has processed over 2 billion interactions, resolving 98% of customer queries in under 44 seconds

**Retail & E-Commerce Stores**

**Dominant Market Share:** Retail and e-commerce hold 21.2% of the total conversational AI market share—the largest of any industry vertical

**Cost Reduction & Efficiency:** 94% of retail companies using customer service AI report reduced operational costs, driving cost per interaction down by 68% (from $4.60 to $1.45)

**Inventory & Personalization:** 40% of businesses utilize AI for automated inventory prediction and management
. Automated post-sale conversational AI engagement boosts repeat customer purchases by 36%

**3. The Macroeconomic Shift:** Post-DeepSeek Market Repricing & Democratization
The Efficiency Disruption: DeepSeek’s release of open-weight reasoning models at a reported ~95% cost discount challenged the industry assumption that AI progress requires brute-force hardware spending

**The Intelligence Price Band:** In response to open-weight competition, commercial providers like OpenAI slashed GPT-5.6 Luna API prices by ~80%
. This created an "intelligence price ceiling" where mid-tier AI capabilities become utility-priced commodities

**Lowering Barriers to Entry:** Plummeting token costs allow mid-sized enterprises and small businesses to harness enterprise-grade AI without multi-million-dollar capital budgets
. In fact, mid-market retailers are deploying AI chatbots 3x faster than enterprise giants, and 58% of analytics deployments for models like Claude occur at small businesses with 10 or fewer employees.
